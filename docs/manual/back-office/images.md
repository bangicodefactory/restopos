# Images

Product photos, your receipt logo, the picture behind the customer display. Until now none of these
could be set: the till knew how to display them and there was no way to put one there.

## Adding an image

Wherever you see an image box — on a product, on the receipts tab of a register's settings, on a
dining room — click it and choose a file. **The upload happens straight away**, not when you save
the form. You will see the picture appear immediately and "Uploading…" underneath while it finishes.

Once it is done, saving the form is what attaches it to the product or the register. If you change
your mind before saving, just pick a different one or clear it.

## What you can upload

**PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF**, up to 8 MB.

**SVG files are refused**, and this is deliberate rather than an oversight. An SVG is not really a
picture — it is a document that can contain instructions, and accepting them would let anyone who
can upload a logo run code in other people's browsers. Export your logo as a PNG instead; for
anything printed on a receipt, a PNG is what you want anyway.

A file whose contents do not match its name is also refused. If a `.png` is not really a PNG, you
will be told rather than finding out later when a receipt prints a blank square.

## The receipt logo

**Register settings → Receipts → Receipt logo.** This prints at the top of every receipt that
register produces.

Thermal printers are black and white and low resolution. A crisp, high-contrast image reproduces
well; a photograph or a subtle gradient turns into a grey smudge. If your logo has fine detail, a
simplified version made for printing is worth the trouble.

## Uploading the same image twice

If you upload a file that is already on the system, it is recognised and reused rather than stored
again. Setting the same logo on five registers uses one file, not five — so replacing it later is
one upload, not five.

## Removing an image

Clear the box and save. The picture disappears from the product or register straight away.

The file itself is kept, because the same image may be in use somewhere else. Nothing you can do
from this screen will make an image vanish from a place you did not expect.

## Who can upload

Uploading is its own permission, separate from editing products or register settings. An upload
endpoint is disk space that can be filled, so it is grantable and revocable on its own — managers
and owners have it by default.
