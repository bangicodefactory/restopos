# Notes on the kitchen screen

A note is anything the kitchen needs to know that is not an item: an allergy, a change to how
something is cooked, a request to hold a dish back. There are two kinds and they behave differently.

## The two kinds of note

**A note on an item** belongs to that item and prints and displays with it. "No basil" sits under
the pasta it was typed against.

**A note on the whole order** belongs to the ticket, not to any one dish. "ALLERGY: no onions" and
"table is in a hurry" are order notes. They appear at the top of the card, above the items.

## A note added after the order was sent

This is the case that matters most, because it is the one that used to go wrong.

When the waiter sends an order to the kitchen and *then* comes back with "actually, they said no
onions", the kitchen is already cooking. The note reaches the screens that are already showing that
order, and the card updates in place — you do not need to touch anything or reload the screen. The
new note appears at the top of the card you are already looking at.

The item list does not change, because nothing about the items changed. Only the note is new.

### Which screens get it

Only the screens already showing that order.

If your station never received the order in the first place — the ticket routes to the grill and you
are on the bar — a note added later does not put a new card on your screen. There is nothing on your
station to amend, and a card with no items on it would be worse than no card at all.

The same rule applies to preparation printers: the amended note prints at the stations that already
printed the ticket, and nowhere else.

## What to do when a note appears

Treat a late note as an interruption, not as a new order. The card is the same card and the items
are the same items — read the note, decide whether what is already on the pass is still correct, and
speak to the front of house if it is not.

An allergy note added after cooking has started is the one case worth stopping for.
