# Opening and closing the till

A *shift* runs from the moment you open the till to the moment you count it up. Every sale, every
cash movement and every reading belongs to exactly one shift, which is what makes the end-of-day
figures mean anything.

## Open the till

Tap the register on the home screen. If a shift is already open — yours or a colleague's on another
device — you join it rather than starting a second one. Two tills in the same restaurant share one
shift.

### If it refuses to open

The till checks the register is usable before it lets you start, and says what is wrong rather than
failing vaguely. The usual causes:

- **No payment method configured.** Someone needs to add at least one in the back office.
- **A payment method in the wrong currency.** Every method must match the register's currency.
- **No tax profile set.**

These are back-office settings, not something you can fix from the till.

## Count the opening float

Before the first sale you confirm what is in the drawer. The field is pre-filled with the closing
count from the last shift, which is usually right — but it is a suggestion, not a fact. **Count the
drawer and correct it if it differs.** The difference between the suggestion and what you enter is
recorded.

Tap the denominations grid to count up note by note instead of doing the arithmetic yourself.

> The shift only gets its number when you confirm the opening float, not when you tap Open. A shift
> you abandon before confirming does not burn a number, so there are no gaps in the sequence.

## Cash in and cash out

Money that enters or leaves the drawer without being a sale — a float top-up, paying a supplier,
taking the day's takings to the safe.

1. Open the drawer menu and choose **Cash in** or **Cash out**.
2. Enter the amount.
3. **Give a reason.** It is required, and it is what makes the end-of-shift count explainable.

You can attribute the movement to an employee or a customer, and print a slip for it.

Every movement for the current shift is listed, with its reason and who recorded it. A movement
entered by mistake can be deleted, but **only by someone with permission** — the delete is recorded
either way.

## Take a reading mid-shift (X-report)

An X-report is a snapshot of the shift so far: what has been sold, the tax breakdown, what each
payment method has taken, and what should be in the drawer right now.

**It changes nothing.** Take one at a shift handover, or whenever you want to check the drawer
without closing up. You can take as many as you like; each one is recorded.

If the till still has sales queued to send, the slip says so — the figures are short by whatever is
waiting.

## Close the till

Closing is deliberately more careful than opening.

1. **Queued sales are sent first.** If the till has anything unsent, it flushes before showing you
   any numbers, so you are never counting against a stale total. If something cannot be sent, you
   are told rather than shown a figure that will change.
2. **Unpaid orders block the close.** Anything still open has to be settled or explicitly carried,
   so a table does not vanish at the end of the night.
3. **Count the drawer.** Enter the counted cash, and the counted amount for each non-cash method.
   The screen shows the expected figure and the difference.
4. **Add a note if the count is off.** Write down why while you still remember.

### When the difference is too large

Every restaurant sets a limit on how far the count may be out. Under the limit, you close normally.
Over it, a **manager has to approve the close** — they enter their PIN on your device and the
approval is recorded against the shift.

Any over or short is recorded as its own line on the shift, not quietly folded into the takings.

### Print the shift report

The closing screen offers the full sales report — the same figures as an X-report, plus the counted
cash and the difference. This is the Z-report: it is the record of a finished shift.

## Things that happen without you doing anything

**Another till closing your shift.** If a colleague closes the shift from their device, your till
notices within a second or two and stops taking sales. Finish what you are ringing up first — you
will be told.

**A sale that arrives after closing.** If a till was offline and pushes a sale belonging to a shift
that has already been closed, the sale is not lost and the closed shift is not reopened. The sale
lands in a *rescue* shift, which is a real shift you can read and close like any other. It exists
so the money is somewhere findable rather than nowhere.

**The shift log.** Openings, closings, readings, cash movements, deleted movements and forced
closes are all recorded in order, with who did them. If the morning after raises a question, the
log is the thing that answers it.
